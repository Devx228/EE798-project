\section{Algorithm and Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Input and output.} Every task is a sequence of integer tokens. Tokens are embedded by a learned
table, processed by the model, and mapped back to vocabulary logits by a linear head whose weights are tied
to the embedding. The output at position $t$ is a distribution over the vocabulary, scored against $y_t$
by \eqref{eq:loss}. There is no other preprocessing: the synthetic data are generated directly as token
indices.

\paragraph{Model architecture.} A model is a stack of $n$ pre-norm residual blocks,
\[
  z \leftarrow z + \mathrm{Mixer}(\mathrm{RMSNorm}(z)), \qquad z \leftarrow z + \mathrm{MLP}(\mathrm{RMSNorm}(z)),
\]
followed by a final RMSNorm and the output head. The MLP is a SwiGLU block (omitted for recall, where the
mixer alone is tested). The mixer of each layer is chosen by a pattern string over the letters
\texttt{A} (attention), \texttt{M} (selective SSM) and \texttt{D} (Gated DeltaNet), e.g.\ \texttt{MA} is an
SSM layer followed by an attention layer. Inside the recurrent mixers the input is projected, passed
through a short causal depthwise convolution (kernel 4) and a SiLU, split into the quantities of
\eqref{eq:ssm} or \eqref{eq:delta}, scanned, normalised and gated by a second projection of the input, as
in Mamba-2 and Gated DeltaNet. Keys and queries of DeltaNet are $L_2$-normalised. Attention uses rotary
position embeddings and, where stated, the same short convolution over its queries, keys and values.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Chunkwise selective scan used for training (one head; batch omitted)}
\label{alg:scan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $x\in\R^{T\times P}$ (already scaled by $\Delta$), decays $a\in\R^{T}$, $B,C\in\R^{T\times N}$, chunk length $L$
\State Pad $T$ to a multiple of $L$ with $a=1$ and $x=B=C=0$ \Comment{padding leaves the state unchanged}
\For{each chunk $c$ \textbf{in parallel}}
  \State $\ell_i \gets \sum_{k\le i}\log|a_k|$, \ $s_i \gets \prod_{k\le i}\operatorname{sgn}(a_k)$ \Comment{within the chunk}
  \State $\mathcal{L}_{ij} \gets s_is_j\,e^{\ell_i-\ell_j}\,\mathbb{1}[j\le i]$
  \State $Y^{\text{diag}}_c \gets \big((C B^\top)\odot \mathcal{L}\big)\, x$ \Comment{intra-chunk term of \eqref{eq:chunk}}
  \State $\Sigma_c \gets \sum_j s_Ls_j e^{\ell_L-\ell_j}\, x_j B_j^\top$ \Comment{state written by chunk $c$}
\EndFor
\State $H_1 \gets 0$
\For{$c = 1,\dots,T/L$ \textbf{sequentially}}
  \State $H_{c+1} \gets s_L e^{\ell_L} H_c + \Sigma_c$ \Comment{one recurrence step per chunk}
\EndFor
\State $Y \gets Y^{\text{diag}} + \operatorname{diag}(s_i e^{\ell_i})\, C H_c^\top$ \Comment{contribution of earlier chunks}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Training procedure.} Training data are sampled afresh at every step from the task generator, so
there is no fixed training set and no example is seen twice; the generalisation gap therefore reflects
the rule learned, not overfitting to examples. We use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} with
$\beta=(0.9,0.98)$, weight decay $0.1$ on weight matrices (not on embeddings, gains or biases), a linear warm-up over 100 steps followed by
cosine decay to 10\% of the peak learning rate, gradient-norm clipping at 1.0, and bfloat16 autocast with
the recurrences accumulated in float32. The recurrent mixers are trained with the chunkwise algorithms
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:scan} and \eqref{eq:ut}, chunk length 64). For recall we additionally stop once the
evaluation accuracy reaches 99\% and record the first step at which it exceeds 90\%.

\paragraph{Inference.} Evaluation uses the parallel forward pass on fixed held-out sets. For generation
each mixer also implements a single-token \texttt{step}: the SSM and DeltaNet update their fixed-size states
by \eqref{eq:ssm} and \eqref{eq:delta}, the rotational SSM additionally carries the cumulative angle
$\Phi_t$, and attention appends to a pre-allocated KV cache. The equality of the step-by-step and parallel
outputs is part of the test suite.

\paragraph{Variants studied.} Starting from these mixers we vary one mechanism at a time: the eigenvalue
range (decay in $(0,1)$ vs.\ $(-1,1)$ for the SSM; $\beta\in(0,1)$ vs.\ $(0,2)$ for DeltaNet), rotational
transitions (\eqref{eq:rot}), the number of Householder steps per token ($n_h=1$ vs.\ 2), the state size
of the SSM ($N=16$ vs.\ 64), and a short convolution in attention.
