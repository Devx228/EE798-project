\section{Qualitative Results}
\label{sec:qual}

\paragraph{Length-generalisation curves.} Figure~\ref{fig:state} plots accuracy against test length for the
baseline models. The shape of each curve is more informative than any single number. A model that learned the
group operation produces a flat line at 1.0 (the negative-eigenvalue SSM and Gated DeltaNet on parity). A model
that learned a shortcut starts high at the training length and slides towards chance as sequences get longer
(Gated DeltaNet with $\beta\in(0,1)$ on parity; every model on $\mathbb{Z}_3$). A model that cannot fit the task
at all stays near chance throughout (most models on $S_3$). The wide shaded band of Gated DeltaNet with
$\beta\in(0,2)$ on $S_3$ shows that its success depends strongly on the random seed.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/state_tracking}
\caption{Length generalisation on state tracking for the baseline models. Colour encodes the architecture family
and line style the eigenvalue range (dashed/square: positive only; solid/circle: extended to negative). Lines show
the mean over 3 seeds and shading the min--max range; the grey band marks the training length.}
\label{fig:state}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The extensions against their predecessors.} Figure~\ref{fig:state-ext} places the two extensions next
to the models they extend. On $S_3$ the effect of the second Householder step is unmistakable: the DeltaProduct
curve stays near 1.0 out to length 256 with a narrow band across seeds, while Gated DeltaNet's curve is lower and
its band spans almost the full range. On $\mathbb{Z}_3$ all four curves fall together. On $\mathbb{Z}_2$ the
rotational SSM's wide band reflects one successful seed and two failures.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/state_tracking_ext}
\caption{Rotational SSM and DeltaProduct (solid, triangles) against the negative-eigenvalue SSM and Gated DeltaNet
(dashed, circles). Mean over 3 seeds; shading: min--max.}
\label{fig:state-ext}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{How generalisation decays with distance from the training length.} Figure~\ref{fig:rotation-length}
shows the rotational SSM on $\mathbb{Z}_3$ for three training lengths. Each curve starts high at its own training
length and decays smoothly towards chance; a model trained at 32 does better at 64 than one trained at 16
(0.877 vs.\ 0.675), but all have collapsed by 512. Accuracy that degrades gradually with the distance from the
training length is characteristic of a solution that tracks the state only approximately, so that the error
accumulates over time.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{figures/rotation_length}
\caption{Rotational SSM on $\mathbb{Z}_3$: accuracy vs.\ test length for three training lengths (ring: the training
length). Shading: min--max over 3 seeds.}
\label{fig:rotation-length}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What the rotational SSM actually learned.} In the first layer, the rotation angle of every state pair
depends only on the current token, so the learned angles can be read directly from the weights.
Figure~\ref{fig:rotation-angles} shows them for a model trained at length 16, which reaches 1.000 accuracy at that
length. Solving $\mathbb{Z}_3$ by rotation requires, in at least one pair, token~1 at $+120^\circ$ and token~2 at
$-120^\circ$ relative to token~0 (or the reverse), with a decay close to 1. Instead, seven of the eight pairs rotate by
angles between $-34^\circ$ and $+73^\circ$, and in the pair with the largest rotation the two tokens receive almost
the same angle ($-136.7^\circ$ and $-134.8^\circ$), so this pair cannot even tell them apart. Part of the memory also
decays (decay between 0.63 and 1.00 per step). The network solved the short task with a combination of small,
fading rotations that encodes recent history, not with the group rotation. This is direct evidence for a
shortcut, consistent with its collapse at longer lengths.

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{figures/rotation_angles}
\caption{Learned first-layer rotation angles of the rotational SSM on $\mathbb{Z}_3$, relative to token~0, one dot
per state pair (training length 16, seed 0). Dashed lines mark the $\pm120^\circ$ rotations that solve the task;
a solution needs token~1 and token~2 on opposite dashed lines within the same pair.}
\label{fig:rotation-angles}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Recall and efficiency curves.} In Figure~\ref{fig:recall} the SSM curves drop like a cliff rather than
gradually: with 16 pairs the small SSM is at 0.991, with 32 pairs at 0.134. Recall behaves like a capacity
threshold, not a slow degradation. In Figure~\ref{fig:eff-decode} the attention cache is a straight line of
slope 1 on log--log axes (linear growth) while the recurrent states are flat lines, which is the whole case for
recurrent models in deployment.
